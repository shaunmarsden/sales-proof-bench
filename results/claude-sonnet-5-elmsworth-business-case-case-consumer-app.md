# Business Case: Expanding the AI Email Drafting Pilot

**Prepared for:** VP review
**Prepared by:** Marcus Webb, Head of Customer Support
**Subject:** Request to extend AI email drafting tool from pilot group to full support team

## 1. Business Case Draft

### Background

Over a three-week period, four support agents used an AI drafting tool to help write customer email replies. This was a small, informal pilot. No formal metrics were tracked before or during it.

### What we know for certain (confirmed facts)

- The pilot ran for three weeks.
- Four agents took part.
- The tool was used specifically for drafting customer email replies.
- No time-tracking, ticket-volume, first-response-time, or resolution-time data was collected before or during the pilot.

### What was said (direct quote, clearly marked as such)

On a follow-up call, Marcus reported the following as his own impression of the pilot:

> "Honestly the team seems to be getting through emails quicker, a few of them mentioned it feels like a real time-saver."

This is a single verbal observation from the pilot lead. It has not been verified against any data and should be read as anecdotal, not measured.

### What this is not (assumptions to avoid stating as fact)

The quote above should not be read as meaning: that email handling time has actually decreased, by any amount; that "a few" agents means most, or all, of the four, it is not stated how many agents made this comment, so it cannot be treated as a team-wide view; that the two agents (or however many) who did not comment felt the same way, felt neutral, or felt negatively.

### The ask

Marcus is requesting budget to roll the tool out from four agents to the full team of twenty.

### Recommendation to the VP

Approve a structured, measured second phase rather than a full rollout based on this pilot alone. Treat the current pilot as a signal worth investigating further, not as proof of value.

## 2. Evidence Gap: What Would Be Needed for a Credible ROI Claim, and What We Have Instead

A credible ROI case for a support tooling change would normally need the following. None of it currently exists.

| Data needed for a credible ROI claim | What the case has instead |
| --- | --- |
| Average email handling time, before and during the pilot | No time-tracking data was collected at all |
| First-response-time metrics, before and during the pilot | Not measured |
| Resolution-time metrics, before and during the pilot | Not measured |
| Ticket or email volume handled per agent, before and during the pilot | Not measured |
| Customer satisfaction scores, before and during the pilot | Not measured, and not something the pilot even attempted to track |
| Adoption rate: how consistently the four agents actually used the tool | Unknown. "A few" agents commented; it is not known if all four used it, or how often |
| Any comparison group (agents not using the tool, over the same period) | None existed. All four pilot agents used the tool, so there is nothing to compare it against |
| Cost of the tool at full scale (20 agents) versus the value it would need to deliver to justify that cost | Not addressed in the source material |

What the case has instead of the above is a single verbal comment from the pilot lead, describing a general impression from an unspecified subset of four agents, made without reference to any tracked data.

This is not sufient grounds for a quantified return-on-investment claim. Any percentage, dollar figure, or hours-saved figure presented at this stage would be invented, not measured, and should not appear in the case.

## 3. Recommended Path: Measuring a Real Baseline Going Forward

Rather than inventing numbers now, the recommendation is to run a second, structured phase designed to actually produce the evidence above.

1. **Define the metrics upfront**, before the next phase starts: average time to draft and send a reply (this may need simple time-stamping or agent self-logging, since no tracking exists today), first-response time, resolution time, ticket or email volume per agent, and a short, simple customer satisfaction check on responses handled with the tool.
2. **Establish a baseline first.** Measure these metrics for a set period using current methods, with no tool, before introducing the tool more broadly.
3. **Run a longer, larger pilot with a comparison group.** Expand to a larger group, for example eight to ten agents, while a similar-sized group continues without the tool over the same period.
4. **Track adoption**, not just outcomes. Record how often and how consistently agents actually use the tool.
5. **Report actual figures after this phase**, whatever they turn out to be, rather than estimating them in advance.
6. **Set a decision point.** Agree in advance what result would justify full rollout to twenty agents.

This path takes longer than approving a full rollout today, but it is the only way to bring the VP a number that will hold up.

## 4. What Must Not Be Presented as Confirmed

Any percentage of time saved. No percentage was measured or stated. Any dollar figure or cost saving. No financial data exists yet. Any specific number of hours saved, per email, per agent, or per week. Any claim that customer satisfaction improved. Satisfaction was never measured during the pilot, and no one involved has said it changed. Any claim that "the team" found the tool useful. Only "a few" agents were reported as commenting, out of four; this must not be generalised to mean the whole pilot group, and certainly not the wider twenty-person team. Any claim that adoption was high or consistent. How the four agents actually used the tool day to day is unknown. Any claim that resolution time, first-response time, or ticket volume changed at all, in either direction. None of these were tracked.

Where the case needs to reference impact, it should say plainly that the pilot produced a positive informal impression from an unspecified number of participants, and that no data currently exists to quantify that impression. The strength of the request to the VP should rest on the case for measuring properly next, not on numbers that do not yet exist.
